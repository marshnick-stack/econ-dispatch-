Turning real policy into assessment material — the safest place to begin

Textbook data is out of date the day it prints. A policy story from this month is not, and students notice the difference — particularly when the policy is one they can see from the classroom window.

This is a worked example of the method: take a real policy, build the stimulus and the questions around it, and you have assessment material that is current, local, and involves no student data whatsoever. Nothing to consent to, nobody to ask, nothing that can go wrong beyond a question that needed rewording.

IB Diploma Programme · Economics · Paper 2

Data response

Answer all parts · 30 marks
Text A — Shanghai's licence plate auction

1 Shanghai controls the number of private cars on its roads by limiting how many new licence plates may be issued each month. Plates are not sold at a fixed price. They are auctioned, and the number available is fixed in advance by the city government, independently of how many people want one.

2 Demand consistently exceeds the quota by a wide margin. Only a small proportion of bidders succeed in any given month, and the average winning bid has remained high enough that the plate can cost a significant fraction of the price of the car itself.

3 The policy is defended on the grounds of congestion and air quality. Each additional car imposes costs on people other than its driver — slower journeys for everyone else, and worse air for those who do not drive at all. Critics argue that the auction favours wealthier households, who can absorb the cost of a plate more easily, and that it has pushed some buyers towards registering vehicles in neighbouring provinces.

4 Plates for battery electric vehicles have been treated differently, with the city issuing them under far less restrictive conditions in order to encourage a shift away from petrol engines.

How This Was Made

The transferable part — roughly twenty minutes

Start with a policy, not a topic. "Externalities" produces a generic paper. A specific policy with a real mechanism produces one with something to argue about. The best stimulus is one where reasonable economists disagree — here, whether a quota or a tax is the better instrument, which is the entire fifteen-mark question.

Make the local example the default, not the treat. This one is visible from any Shanghai classroom window. Students who have watched a parent bid for a plate arrive with an intuition the textbook cannot give them, and evaluation is where that intuition pays.

Write the command terms last. Build the stimulus first, then ask what it can genuinely support. A table with four rows can support a calculation; it cannot support a question about trends over a decade. Most weak practice questions are ones the data cannot actually answer.

Then check it yourself. Every question here has to be answerable from the text and table alone. That check is the part that stays human — and it is quick, because you wrote the stimulus and you know what is in it.

Two honest notes on this example

The figures in Table 1 are illustrative, in the ordinary way of practice materials — they are the right shape for the policy but should not be quoted as data. For a real sitting, drop in the current published figures; the questions do not change.

And check the mark allocations against the current subject guide before you use it. The structure follows the familiar 30-mark data response shape, but the guide is the authority and I am not.

Why this is the safest starting point

Nothing on this page involves a single student. There is no personal information, so there is no cross-border transfer question, no consent to obtain, and nobody who can be harmed if the output is poor. The worst case is a question you rewrite.

That is exactly why it is first. You will learn what these tools are genuinely good at on work where being wrong costs you nothing — and by the time you reach the tasks that do involve student data, you will already know which parts to keep for yourself. The other two starting points →
